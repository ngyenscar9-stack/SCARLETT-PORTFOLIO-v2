HopeBox

A Design Thinking project focused on one business challenge: how HopeBox could build more consistent corporate engagement beyond seasonal campaigns and strengthen year-round revenue resilience.

01 / Business Problem

Revenue was not the whole problem.

HopeBox operates with year-round programme and support costs, while corporate purchasing and engagement are concentrated around Tet, Mid-Autumn and Christmas. The project reframed that instability as a relationship problem: corporate partners had limited reasons and structures to stay engaged between seasonal peaks.

01

The strategic shift

Instead of treating the challenge as a short-term sales dip, the team focused on creating continuous corporate touchpoints that could support stronger partnership continuity and more stable revenue.

02 / Stakeholder Insight

Prioritise the people who shape continuity.

Corporate B2B partners were prioritised because procurement decisions, CSR budgets and long-term partnership commitment directly affect HopeBox's revenue continuity. Stakeholder mapping and empathy work highlighted a consistent need for measurable impact, clear engagement opportunities and stronger year-round visibility.

03 / Decision Logic

Four routes. One practical quick win.

The ideation stage produced four solution routes across continuous engagement and strategic partnership. An Action Priority Matrix then compared implementation effort with strategic impact. The Mid-Year Impact Gala was selected as the strongest quick win.

Why the gala?

It creates a dedicated mid-year reason for corporate partners to reconnect with HopeBox during a low-engagement period, while combining fundraising, networking, impact storytelling and public recognition in one experience.

What it unlocks

The event becomes more than a one-off activation. It creates a platform for deeper ESG conversations, sponsorship pathways and follow-up communication that can extend beyond the physical event.

04 / Prototype System

A concept built across the full partner journey.

The proposal was translated into a set of connected artefacts rather than a single event visual. The prototype system included the gala experience, partnership collateral, impact storytelling and post-event digital recognition.

05 / Testing Framework

Test the idea before treating it as a solution.

The final stage defined how the concept should be evaluated using three Design Thinking lenses. The framework connects audience response, financial sustainability and operational capacity to measurable indicators.

01

Desirability

RSVP intention, sponsor interest, storytelling response and audience feedback test whether the experience is meaningful enough to attract corporate and community engagement.

02

Viability

Sponsorship uptake, donation conversion, fundraising efficiency and repeat partnership interest test whether the concept can contribute to sustainable revenue.

03

Feasibility

Logistics, staff coordination, production requirements and low-resource digital follow-up test whether HopeBox can realistically deliver and repeat the model.

Important: these are proposed evaluation measures from the project, not live campaign performance results.
