01 / Business Opportunity

Turn a brand value
into a service.

voco Scenia Bay already positions itself around coastal calm, personalisation and indulgent rest. The opportunity was not to invent a new brand promise, but to make that promise operational before the guest even arrived.

RU responds to a personalisation gap by moving comfort preferences upstream, so the room can be prepared around the guest rather than waiting for requests during the stay.

The strategic bridge

RU sits between voco Scenia Bay's “The New Rhythm at the Bay” positioning and a more personalised guest experience. The concept makes the brand promise tangible through a pre-arrival sleep profile.

Branding direction
I developed the name RU — The Rhythm of Rest to echo the hotel's “New Rhythm” language while grounding the idea in the Vietnamese word ru, associated with lulling and gentle rest.

03 / The Concept

Rest, prepared
before arrival.

RU is a pre-arrival sleep profile that captures how a guest prefers to wind down before they reach the hotel. The service combines six personalisation choices: pillowcase, scent, evening tea, room temperature, lighting and sound.

The result is not a larger amenity menu. It is a prepared environment, designed to reduce guest effort and make personalisation feel built into the stay.

04 / Why It Works

Less guest effort.
More meaningful rest.

The strategic case moved beyond “people like sleep”. We examined how guests already compensate for comfort gaps, how conventional hotel personalisation remains reactive, and how established hospitality players structure sleep experiences.

The key insight was simple: the need is not wider choice. It is lower effort.

05 / Digital Experience

Don't just explain it.
Let them experience it.

I designed and built the live RU digital experience to make the service tangible beyond slides. During the client pitch, the prototype became a focal point for showing how the pre-arrival journey could actually feel to a guest.

The live build also gave the concept a stronger “real product” quality, rather than leaving it as a hypothetical service proposal.
